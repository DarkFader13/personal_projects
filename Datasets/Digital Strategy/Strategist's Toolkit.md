## Understanding Business Strategy
### 1. **Definition of Business Strategy:
   - Defined by mission, plan, and actions.
### 2. **Importance of Strategic Analysis:**
   - Strategic analysis crucial for assessing strategy viability.
   - Not exclusive to CEOs; relevant to various stakeholders.
### 3. **The Strategist's Challenge:**
   - Balancing values, opportunities, and capabilities for competitive positions.
### 4. **Economic Profits in Competitive Markets:**
   - Definition: Returns exceeding opportunity cost of capital.
   - Perfectly competitive markets yield no economic profits.
### 5. **Real-world Market Dynamics:**
   - Rarely perfect; firms often possess competitive advantage.
   - Role of the strategist: identify sources of competitive advantage.
### 6. **Capturing Economic Profits:**
   - Firms achieve through barriers to competition or imitation.
  
## Exploring the SWOT Analysis Framework

### Introduction to SWOT Analysis:
Acronym: Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats.
### Purpose of SWOT Analysis:    
- Highlights two main areas in strategic analysis:
	- External competitive environment (Opportunities, Threats).
    - Internal firm capabilities (Strengths, Weaknesses).
### Understanding SWOT Analysis:
- Framework often presented with bullet point lists for each category.
- Encouragement to focus on listing areas rather than rigidly categorizing them.
### Deeper Analysis with Other Tools:    
- SWOT is an overarching framework.
- Utilize additional tools for deeper insights:
    - Capability analysis, environmental analysis, five forces analysis for external environment.
    - Capabilities analysis, strategy maps for internal strengths and weaknesses.

## Understanding Environmental Analysis in Strategy

### 1. **Importance of Environmental Analysis:**    
- Core analysis alongside competitor analysis.
- Focuses on broader competitive environment and industry trends.
### 2. **Key Categories in Environmental Analysis:**
- Demographic Trends: Population changes and their impact on demand.
- Social-Cultural Influences: Trends in fashion and social behaviors.
- Technological Developments: Impacts of new technologies on the industry.
- Macroeconomic Impacts: Influence of business cycles on demand and competition.
- Political-Legal Pressures: Regulatory and legal factors affecting the industry.
- Global Trade Trends: Effects of globalization and trade agreements on competition.
### 3. **Purpose of Environmental Analysis:**
- Identifies trends and factors shaping the competitive landscape.
- Helps strategists anticipate changes and adapt accordingly.
### 4. **Approach to Environmental Analysis:**
- Typically presented as a bullet-pointed list for each category.
- Aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of the competitive environment.


## Five Forces Analysis Overview:
- Tool to assess competitive forces impacting industry profitability and margins.
- Helps understand industry structure and competitive dynamics.
- Doesn't cover individual firm differences but crucial for strategic analysis.
### Components of Five Forces Analysis:
- #### Threat of New Entrants:
	- Incumbent firms' strong competitive advantage reduces threat.
	- Credible retaliation deters potential new entrants.
- #### Threat of Substitutes:
	- Low threat when switching costs are high.
	- Low cross-price elasticity reduces threat.
- #### Bargaining Power of Buyers / Suppliers:
	- Depends on relative concentration of buyers/suppliers.
	- Viability of alternatives affects bargaining power.
	- Transparency of information impacts bargaining power.
- #### Intensity of Rivalry:
	- More competitors increase rivalry.
	- Larger incentives to fight intensify rivalry.
	- Difficulty in tacit coordination increases rivalry.
### Purpose of Five Forces Analysis:    
- Assess long-term profitability potential of an industry or market segment.
- Focuses on industry/market segment, not individual firms.
- Doesn't cover differences between firms or individual capabilities.
### Scoping the Industry:    
- Depends on breadth or narrowness of analysis.
- Can analyze broadly (e.g., all manufacturers) or narrowly (e.g., manufacturers of specific product).
- Experimenting with different scopes yields varied insights.
### Additional Considerations:
- Role of complements and institutional environment not explicitly discussed.
- Aim is to understand industry dynamics and structure for strategic insights.


## Competitive Advantage, Positioning, and Strategy

### Competitive Advantage 

**1. Understanding Capabilities:**
- Analogous to SWOT's strengths and weaknesses assessment.
- Arises from interactions of people, processes, and systems.
- Essential to identify key components.

**2. Building Sustainable Advantage:**
- Alignment internally and externally crucial.
- Value creation for stakeholders is paramount.
- Requirement for durability and difficulty of imitation.

**3. Acquiring Superior Capabilities:**
- Acquisition or development options.
- Necessity for superior know-why or know-how.
- Limited role of luck in capability acquisition.

**4. Core Principle of Business Strategy:**
- Challenge of value creation if capabilities easily replicable.


### Competitive Positioning

**1. Positioning Defined:**
- Market positions shaped by both needs and delivery methods.
- Focus on practicality alongside conceptualization.

**2. Diverse Profitable Positions:**
- Industries offer varied profitable positions.
- Differentiated or niche positions can be as profitable as cost leadership.
- Execution strategies vary for each position.

**3. Factors for Profitable Positions:**
- Reliance on favorable industry structure and superior capabilities.
- Understanding market dynamics and internal strengths crucial.

**4. Combination Strategies:**
- Firms can pursue multiple positions.
- Caution against getting stuck in the middle.
- Strong strategic logic needed for combining objectives.

**5. Core Challenge of Strategy:**
- Capturing valuable, defendable, competitive positions.
- Essence of strategic decision-making.

### Competitive Landscapes: The Strategy Map Advantage

**1. Purpose of a Strategy Map:**
- Understanding competitive positioning within an industry.
- Combines competitor analysis with capabilities assessment.

**2. Utility of a Strategy Map:**
- Complements other strategic analysis tools.
- Visual representation aids in comparison and differentiation.
- Widely used in consulting for its versatility.

**3. Constructing a Strategy Map:**
- Select criteria/factors to map competitors.
- Horizontal and vertical axes represent different factors.
- Size of circles indicates the firm's competitive scope.

**4. Example: Automobile Industry in the Early 2000s:**
- Criteria: Average vehicle cost, sportiness, and variety of models.
- Visualization of how firms differ in competitive positions.

**5. Determining Key Criteria:**
- Criteria selection depends on industry and context.
- Trial and error process to identify relevant factors.
- Market share, R&D spending, quality, among others, are potential criteria.

**Conclusion:**
- Strategy map aids in understanding competitive positions.
- Iterative process of criteria selection leads to insightful visualizations.
- Valuable addition to strategic analysis toolkit for industry assessment.
